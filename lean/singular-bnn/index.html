The geometry of a low-rank posterior.

Three results that explain why a distribution over low-rank factors induces a singular distribution over weight matrices.

PaperLean sourceReproduce the verification
1. Constrained support2. Zero Lebesgue measure3. Singularity

Setting. Let $A\in\mathbb R^{m\times r}$ and $B\in\mathbb R^{n\times r}$, let $q$ be any probability law on $(A,B)$, and let $q_W$ be its pushforward under $(A,B)\mapsto AB^\top$.

The factors need not be independent or Gaussian. Define $\mathcal R_r=\{W:\operatorname{rank}(W)\le r\}$; $\lambda$ denotes Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb R^{m\times n}$.

01 / Paper: Constrained Support lemma

The induced posterior stays in the low-rank set.

$$\operatorname{rank}(AB^\top)\le r,\qquad q_W(\mathcal R_r)=1.$$

This holds for every factor law, including dependent and discrete distributions.

Proof. The rank of a product is at most the rank of its first factor, and $A$ has $r$ columns. Thus every factor pair maps into $\mathcal R_r$. Its pushforward assigns probability one to that set.

02 / Paper: Measure Zero of $\mathcal R_r$ lemma

The low-rank set has zero ambient volume.

$$r<\min(m,n)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\lambda(\mathcal R_r)=0.$$

The condition is essential: if $r\ge\min(m,n)$, every $m$-by-$n$ matrix already has rank at most $r$.

Proof. Every $(r+1)$-by-$(r+1)$ minor vanishes on $\mathcal R_r$. Choose the leading minor: its determinant is a nonzero polynomial, as an identity block makes it equal to one. The formalization proves that a nonzero polynomial has a Lebesgue-null zero set by induction on its number of variables, using finite univariate root sets and Fubini's theorem. The low-rank set is contained in this null set.

03 / Paper: Singularity of the Induced Posterior theorem

The induced posterior is singular to Lebesgue measure.

$$r<\min(m,n)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q_W\perp\lambda.$$

All posterior mass lies on a set that has zero Lebesgue measure in the full weight space.

Proof. The zero set of the chosen minor is closed, hence measurable. Its Lebesgue measure is zero, while its complement has zero induced-posterior measure. These two sets witness mutual singularity. The Lean theorem actually establishes this for any measure on the factor space, without requiring total mass one.
